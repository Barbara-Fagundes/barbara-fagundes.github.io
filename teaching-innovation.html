Teaching Innovation & Impact

Documenting instructional decisions, pedagogical innovation, and professional growth.

This portfolio documents my instructional practices, decisions, collaborations, and ongoing development as an engineering and computing educator. It creates space to examine what I choose, why I choose it, and what the available evidence can—and cannot—tell me about those choices.

Instructional Decision-Making

03 / Claims and their support

Evidence of Impact

Contributions and learning outcomes require different kinds of evidence. This collection keeps those distinctions visible and records the limits of each claim.

Implementation

What was done?

A dated material or record can document an activity implemented.

Adoption

What was taken up?

A documented use of a practice can establish adoption, with its scope specified.

Learning improvement

What changed?

Appropriate learning evidence and analysis are needed to support improvement; participation or positive feedback alone is insufficient.

05 / Connecting practice and inquiry

Research Connection: Collaborative Self-Study

Study in development · No findings presented

Learning to Make Instructional Decisions in First-Year Engineering: A Collaborative Self-Study

This study examines:

  1. How I navigate instructional decisions across two first-year engineering courses with different curricular and collaborative structures.
  2. How these experiences, including mentoring and professional development, inform my understanding of my teaching philosophy.

Reflections can be organized by date, course, teaching incident, alternatives, constraints, guidance, and emerging themes. Contemporaneous accounts should remain distinguishable from retrospective interpretations.

Research documentation is maintained separately from this public portfolio. Completed journals, identifiable student records, assessment data, confidential communications, and private mentor feedback belong in an approved restricted research environment. A portfolio entry is not automatically research data; applicable ethical and IRB requirements must be determined separately.
