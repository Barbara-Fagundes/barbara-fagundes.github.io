Learning Sciences & Computing Education Research · Case Study

Computational Thinking in K-2 Classrooms

NSF-funded research · RECT Project & PictureSTEM · Purdue University · 2018–2023

1. Overview

As part of two NSF-funded projects at Purdue University — the RECT Project and PictureSTEM — I contributed to research examining how young children in K-2 classrooms develop foundational computational thinking (CT) concepts through structured learning activities, educational robotics, and engineering-integrated curriculum.

This work generated peer-reviewed publications, curriculum resources deployed in multiple Indiana classrooms, and assessment instruments for measuring CT in early elementary learners.

2. Challenge

Computational thinking is increasingly recognized as a foundational 21st-century skill — but how do very young children (ages 5–8) actually develop CT concepts like sequencing, algorithm design, debugging, and pattern recognition? Most existing frameworks were designed for older learners, and there was limited evidence about what CT learning looks like in K-2 settings.

The projects aimed to fill that gap: designing age-appropriate learning experiences, studying their implementation in real classrooms, and producing evidence that could guide curriculum design and teacher practice.

3. Audience

First and second-grade students (ages 6–8) and their classroom teachers across multiple elementary schools in Indiana. Secondary audiences included curriculum designers, pre-service educators, and K-12 STEM education researchers.

4. My Role

  • Managed large-scale classroom video data collection across Indiana school sites
  • Conducted video-based qualitative analysis of student CT behaviors and problem-solving strategies
  • Co-designed and piloted CT learning activities using educational robotics (Bee-Bot, Code-a-Pillar)
  • Contributed to the development and validation of CT assessment instruments for K-2 learners
  • Co-authored peer-reviewed publications in computing education and engineering education journals
  • Presented findings at ASEE, SIGCSE, and SITE International Conference

5. Research Process

Literature Review → Curriculum Co-Design → Classroom Implementation → Video Data Collection → Analysis → Publication

6. CT Concepts Studied

Sequencing

How students construct and interpret ordered sets of instructions to direct a robot or complete a task.

Algorithm Design

How students plan, refine, and communicate multi-step solutions to engineering design problems.

Debugging

How students identify and correct errors in their programs or instruction sequences.

Pattern Recognition

How students identify repeating structures and apply them to simplify complex tasks.
