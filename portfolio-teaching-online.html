Instructional Design & E-Learning Development · Case Study

Teaching Engineering Online

Graduate course co-development at Purdue University · Brightspace · 2024

1. Overview

In 2024, I co-developed a graduate-level course on online engineering pedagogy at Purdue University in collaboration with Professor Audeen Fentiman (Crowley Family Professor in Engineering Education). The course was designed to equip engineering instructors and graduate students with the theory, tools, and practical skills to design and deliver effective online and blended learning experiences.

My contributions spanned the full instructional design process: from defining learning objectives and mapping the course structure to writing instructional scripts, developing multimedia-ready content, designing assessments, and ensuring accessibility throughout all course materials.

2. Challenge

The challenge was to translate a broad and evolving body of knowledge about online teaching into a structured, practical, and accessible learning experience — one that would be relevant both to graduate students with limited teaching experience and to practicing instructors seeking to improve their online courses.

The course needed to balance theory and application, move learners through meaningful practice, and model the same design principles it was teaching.

3. Audience

Graduate students and engineering faculty enrolled in a graduate-level engineering education course at Purdue University. Learners ranged from doctoral students with limited teaching experience to current instructors navigating online and hybrid course delivery for the first time.

4. My Role

I contributed to the instructional design and development of the course materials, working closely with Professor Fentiman in an iterative, collaborative process. My specific contributions included:

  • Writing measurable learning objectives for each module
  • Designing the 5-week module structure and content sequencing
  • Developing instructional scripts for video-based content
  • Creating slide decks, module outlines, and learner-facing materials
  • Designing formative and summative assessments aligned to objectives
  • Applying accessibility standards across multimedia and written content
  • Iterating on materials based on instructor feedback and course needs

5. Design Process

Analyze → Design → Map → Prototype → Develop → Iterate → Deliver

The process began with a needs analysis — identifying the audience's prior knowledge, course context, and learning environment constraints. From there, I mapped the course structure using backward design, starting with desired outcomes and working backward to content and assessments. Prototypes of individual modules were reviewed and revised collaboratively before full development.
