Faculty Development & Student Partnership · Case Study

Student Pedagogy Advocates

Center for Instructional Excellence, Purdue University · 2024–2025

1. Overview

The Student Pedagogy Advocates (SPA) program is a student-faculty partnership initiative at Purdue University's Center for Instructional Excellence (CIE). The program embeds undergraduate student perspectives directly into instructional improvement processes — creating structured opportunities for students to work alongside faculty, provide candid feedback, and contribute to evidence-based teaching practices.

I co-led the program design, mentored undergraduate student advocates, facilitated workshops, and co-authored a conference paper disseminating the SPA model (Fagundes, Guberman, Smart & Holder, 2025, ASEE). doi:10.18260/1-2--55986

2. Challenge

Faculty often lack direct, candid feedback from students about what's working and what isn't in their courses — and when they do receive it, it's typically through end-of-semester evaluations that arrive too late to act on.

The SPA program created a proactive, structured mechanism for student voices to inform teaching practice in real time, while also developing undergraduate students' capacity to think critically about pedagogy and learning.

3. Audience

Undergraduate student advocates, faculty participants seeking instructional support, and graduate student collaborators within Purdue's Center for Instructional Excellence. The program model was designed to be transferable to other institutional contexts.

4. My Role

  • Co-designed the SPA program model, including partner roles, session structure, and feedback protocols
  • Recruited, trained, and mentored undergraduate student advocates
  • Facilitated structured observation and debrief sessions between student advocates and faculty
  • Documented program processes and outcomes for institutional reporting
  • Co-authored and presented a conference paper at ASEE 2025 describing the program model and early findings

5. Program Process

Program Design → Partner Recruitment → Training → Facilitation → Documentation → Evaluation → Dissemination

6. Outcomes

Student voice integrated into instructional improvement

Undergraduate advocates provided structured, actionable feedback to faculty during the semester — enabling mid-course corrections rather than post-hoc evaluations.

Undergraduate development as pedagogical thinkers

Student advocates reported deeper understanding of course design, learning theory, and the complexity of teaching — skills that transferred to their own academic and professional contexts.

Peer-reviewed dissemination of the model

The program design and early findings were shared with the broader engineering and STEM education community through a conference paper at ASEE 2025.
