AI Education Research & Learning Intervention · Case Study

AI-Lab: Generative AI in Computing Courses

Completed qualitative study at Purdue University · 800+ students · arXiv preprint 2025

1. Overview

The AI-Lab Study examined how undergraduate students in computer science and engineering courses adopt and ethically use generative AI tools — and how a structured instructional intervention (the AI-Lab) changed that behavior. I led the qualitative research component of this large-scale study at Purdue University, collaborating with faculty from Computer Science and a data analyst.

Findings were published as an arXiv preprint: Evaluating the AI-Lab intervention: Impact on student perception and use of generative AI in early undergraduate computer science courses (Dickey, Bejarano, Kuperus & Fagundes, 2025). doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.00100

2. Challenge

As generative AI tools became widely used in academic settings, instructors and institutions faced a significant gap: they had limited evidence about how students were actually using these tools, whether responsible use was occurring, and what instructional design choices could shape that behavior.

The AI-Lab intervention was designed to close that gap — but evaluating whether it worked required a rigorous qualitative approach capable of capturing the nuance in students' reasoning, practices, and perceptions.

3. Audience

Undergraduate students enrolled in early computer science and engineering courses at Purdue University — over 800 students across multiple course sections. Secondary audiences included course instructors, computing education researchers, and institutions designing AI literacy programs.

4. My Role

I designed and led the qualitative components of the study:

  • Developed focus group and interview protocols aligned to study objectives
  • Facilitated student focus groups before and after the AI-Lab intervention
  • Conducted thematic analysis using NVivo, developing and applying a coding framework
  • Synthesized qualitative findings into evidence-based instructional recommendations
  • Contributed to the arXiv manuscript including methods, findings, and discussion sections

5. Research Process

Scoping → Protocol Design → IRB Approval → Data Collection → Thematic Analysis → Synthesis → Dissemination

The study used a pre/post design with focus groups conducted before and after the AI-Lab intervention. Transcripts were analyzed using thematic analysis following Braun and Clarke's framework, with codes developed inductively and refined through team-based review.

6. Key Findings

Student use shifted from uncritical to strategic

After the intervention, students described more intentional, reflective patterns of AI tool use — checking outputs, citing limitations, and integrating tools into their workflow rather than relying on them passively.

Responsible use requires explicit instruction

Students reported that without structured guidance, they defaulted to whatever felt most efficient. The AI-Lab created a scaffold for thinking about ethics, accuracy, and attribution.

Instructor framing matters

Students were highly sensitive to how instructors framed AI tool use in their courses. Ambiguity or silence on the topic was interpreted as permission or prohibition — neither of which supported responsible learning.
