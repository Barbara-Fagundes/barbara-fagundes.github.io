Page title:
Barbara Fagundes | AI Learning and Cognitive Outcomes

Header:
Barbara Fagundes, Ph.D.
Studying how people think, learn, and make decisions when using AI systems

Section label:
AI Learning Research Direction

Intro text:
I am a learning scientist studying how students reason, seek help, and rely on AI in real learning environments. My work aims to understand not just whether people use AI, but how AI changes learning processes, decision-making, and meaningful cognitive outcomes over time.

Best Match Themes:
- Human learning, reasoning, and behavioral research
- Qualitative and mixed-methods study design
- Human-AI interaction in real educational settings
- Translation from evidence to applied recommendations

Research:
From AI use to learning and cognitive outcomes

Research text:
My research has examined how undergraduate students use generative AI in engineering and computer science courses, with attention to reasoning, decision-making, help-seeking, and patterns of reliance on AI-generated outputs.

I am especially interested in questions about autonomy, deeper learning, transfer, productive support, and the conditions under which AI helps or hinders the development of capability.

That work also connects to a broader education question I care about: how universities can help K-12 systems build AI literacy in ways that are technically informed, educationally meaningful, and ethically responsible.

Focus:
Core strengths
- Learning science and behavioral research
- AI in education and human-AI interaction
- Real-world studies in complex learning settings
- Evidence translation for product and teaching contexts

Methods:
Research methods and data practices
- Focus groups and interviews
- Qualitative and interpretive analysis
- Behavioral analysis of learning processes
- NVivo coding and thematic analysis
- Survey design and qualitative plus basic quantitative analysis

Questions:
What I study in practice
- How students interpret and integrate AI outputs
- How help-seeking and overreliance appear in use patterns
- How AI shapes reasoning, problem decomposition, and autonomy
- How AI literacy can be supported across educational settings
- How findings can inform better learning and AI-supported experiences
